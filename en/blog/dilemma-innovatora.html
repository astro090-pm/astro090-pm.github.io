The Innovator's Dilemma by Clayton Christensen: why strong companies lose to newcomers

22 March 2020booksproduct

Key ideas from the book on disruptive innovation: why well-managed companies that do everything right lose their market to simpler, cheaper products.

Clayton Christensen was a professor at Harvard Business School. The book came out in 1997 and introduced the term "disruptive innovation". The paradox he describes: market leaders lose not because of bad management but precisely because they follow good management practice.

Sustaining and disruptive technologies

  • Sustaining technologies improve the product along the dimensions the best customers already value. Leaders almost always win this race.
  • Disruptive technologies are initially worse on the main dimensions but simpler, cheaper or more convenient. They come in at the low end or create a new market where the old metrics don't matter.

Why leaders don't respond

  • The best customers don't ask for a simple, cheap product — they ask for improvements.
  • The new market is too small to move a big company's revenue.
  • Margins are lower, and resources flow to where margins are higher.
  • Meanwhile the disruptive technology improves faster than mainstream needs grow — and one day it becomes "good enough".

Christensen shows this with hard disk drives, excavators and steel: each new generation of technology came in from the edge of the market.

What to do

  • Put the disruptive project into a separate small team for which a small market is a big success.
  • Expect the first guess about the market to be wrong and plan for cheap attempts.
  • Look for new markets and new customers instead of selling the novelty to current ones.

What a PM can take from it

Check whether the product is "overshooting" customers with features they no longer need, and watch cheap alternatives that look unserious today.

Who should read it

PMs and leaders in mature companies — to see threats; and startup founders — to understand a big player's blind spot.
