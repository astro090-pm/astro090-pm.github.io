I'm teaching an IT management course at a university

16 October 2026teachingmanagementproduct

This autumn I teach an "IT Management" course to first-year students: seven lectures on Tuesdays, from 13 October to 24 November. The first lecture has already taken place.

Why I do it

For six years I've been launching products in the public sector, telecom, retail and banking. I've learned that IT projects usually break not in the code but in management: the team doesn't know which problem it solves, the client asks for one thing and gets another, timelines and budgets slip. I want students to see this before they run into it themselves.

Syllabus

  1. Introduction to IT management: roles, life cycle, the product manager
  2. IT product, idea and MVP: from hypothesis to the first version
  3. Agile, Scrum and Kanban
  4. Product metrics and analytics
  5. Experiments and economics: A/B tests and unit economics
  6. Launch and growth: marketing, release and risks
  7. Strategy: vision, OKRs and roadmap

How the course works

Little theory — mostly real cases: Sberbank's move to Agile, Dodo Pizza as an IT company, Yandex Lavka's "duct-tape" MVP, Gosuslugi's growth without revenue, the life cycle of ICQ.

Students work in teams of 5–6 and run one service throughout the course: medicine delivery, grocery delivery, gym booking, coffee pre-orders, online clinic booking or a pizzeria's own delivery. Each option has a built-in conflict: marketing wants to launch everywhere at once, IT says the system won't cope. After each lecture there's a case: build a team, frame a hypothesis and a cheap way to test it, cut the MVP, list the risks, pick the metrics. At the end — a final project: their own product from problem to metrics.

About AI

A separate block covers how a product manager becomes an AI product manager. Job postings requiring AI skills in Russia grew 2.7× in a year, and most AI PMs didn't come from computer science. My advice to students: use AI in study and projects, understand where models fail, and keep the fundamentals — the user, value and metrics.
