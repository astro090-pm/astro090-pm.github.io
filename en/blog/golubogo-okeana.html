Blue Ocean Strategy by Kim and Mauborgne: how to escape competition

9 October 2022booksmanagement

Key ideas from the book about not fighting competitors over an existing market but creating new market space where there is no competition yet.

W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne are professors at INSEAD. The book is based on a study of strategic moves by companies across industries over more than a hundred years.

Red and blue oceans

  • A red ocean is an existing market with known rules where companies take share from each other. The more players, the lower the profits.
  • A blue ocean is a market that doesn't exist yet. Demand is created rather than fought over.

Value innovation

The main idea is not to choose between differentiation and low cost but to pursue both. That's possible if you drop what the industry considers essential but customers don't need.

The strategy canvas and four actions

The strategy canvas shows the factors an industry competes on and the level each player offers. To draw a new value curve, the authors propose four questions:

  • Eliminate — which factors the industry takes for granted should be eliminated?
  • Reduce — what can be reduced well below the industry standard?
  • Raise — what should be raised well above the standard?
  • Create — what should be created that the industry has never offered?

The classic example is Cirque du Soleil: a circus without animal acts and star performers, but with a storyline and artistic production, like theatre.

Noncustomers

New markets usually hide among noncustomers: those about to leave, those who consciously refuse the industry, and those who've never considered it. The question isn't "how do we win customers from competitors" but "why do these people use no one at all".

What a PM can take from it

Draw the strategy canvas for your category and go through the four questions: often the most valuable decision is to remove what all competitors do out of habit.

Who should read it

PMs and leaders stuck in a feature race with competitors. The book gives simple tools for a strategy session with the team.
