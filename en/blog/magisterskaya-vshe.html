My HSE master's thesis — how to tell whether digital transformation will pay off

30 June 2021researchfintechmanagement

My master's thesis in the Corporate Finance programme at HSE University's Faculty of Economic Sciences was "Modelling and evaluating the efficiency of a company's digital transformation investment projects". It started the research line I now continue in my PhD at RANEPA — this time about AI.

The problem

Large companies invest huge sums in digital transformation, yet there are hardly any methods to forecast a project's success and estimate future benefits upfront. Existing evaluations:

  • rely on qualitative criteria without clear ways to measure them;
  • count only financial indicators and don't split capital and operating costs by technology area;
  • report final effects without a calculation method, so the same indicators differ between sources.

What I studied

  • Analysed the digital transformation programmes of Rosseti ("Digital Transformation 2030"), Russian Railways ("Digital Railway") and Lukoil ("Digital Lukoil") and compared their technology bets: IoT, AI, big data, digital twins, robots and drones.
  • Separated two concepts: digital optimisation improves existing processes, while digital transformation changes the value proposition and creates new products.
  • Compared groups of evaluation methods — from cost accounting (TCO) to the balanced scorecard — and proposed steps for defining a project's non-financial metrics.
  • Built a forecasting evaluation model with elements of risk scenario analysis.

Four hypotheses

  1. Digital transformation projects are innovation projects and must be evaluated differently from ordinary investments: goals, timelines and costs are not fixed upfront.
  2. A company with steady growth can keep its competitive advantages for a while even without digital transformation.
  3. With outdated infrastructure, lack of funds and unprepared staff, transformation leads to wasted spending and missed results.
  4. A successful transformation project changes the business process model and makes the company more efficient.

Conclusion

A digital transformation project alone doesn't guarantee success. Management must understand at the review stage how realistic the project is and whether the company is ready for it at its current life-cycle stage. But ignoring digitalisation is even more dangerous: technology players take over markets. That's why a comprehensive evaluation method is needed, with non-financial metrics next to financial ones.

I now study the same question — "is the company ready, and what should be prepared upfront" — for AI: see the talk on infrastructure requirements and the research page.
