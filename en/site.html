This website as a product

A website is a product too. It has users with different jobs, hypotheses and decisions to make — including decisions to remove things.

Who it is for and why

WhoJobWhat the site offers
Hiring managers and HRquickly judge whether the candidate fitsCV, results in numbers, a summary at the top of every case, the “I'm hiring” route on the home page
Studentsunderstand the profession and learnthe IT management course, lecture slides, book reviews
Colleaguesexchange experiencemy path in AI agents, research, open-source project code

Principles

  • Verifiable facts only: certificates link to verification, projects to code or a case, articles to the publication.
  • Numbers with context: where a result spans two years, that is stated.
  • Every audience gets a short path to what it came for.

What was done

  • “Who are you?” routes on the home page — for hiring managers, students and colleagues.
  • A summary at the top of every case: main result, role, where and when.
  • A “What I learned” section at the end of cases.
  • Certificates with thumbnails and verification links.
  • PDFs open in a side panel without leaving the page.
  • A full English version, serverless search, dark theme.

Decision log: what I removed and why

WhatDecision
Stats strip on the home pageRemoved: the numbers stay in the CV and case summaries, and the first screen got calmer.
“Random post” buttonRemoved: it helped none of the three audiences.
“Presentations” sectionDissolved: each PDF now sits next to its project, case or post.
“Activity by month” blockRemoved from the Timeline: pretty, but explained nothing.
Career line in the headerRemoved: the same information is on the Timeline in more detail.
A list of 50 booksReplaced with a list of books I have read, with reviews.
A big redesign with “coaching” and “students” pagesPublished and rolled back the same day: the structure diluted the main message; a few ideas from it stayed.

Metrics

The site does not collect visit statistics yet, so the decisions above are qualitative, not data-driven. The next step is to add a counter and check which routes and sections people actually use.

How it is built

A static Python generator with no frameworks: posts and cases are written in Markdown, pages are built by a script and published on GitHub Pages. The English version is built from the same templates. I build the site together with an AI coding assistant. Site code on GitHub.

What's next

  • An own domain instead of the GitHub address.
  • Testimonials from colleagues and students — with their consent.
  • A visit counter and data-driven decisions.
