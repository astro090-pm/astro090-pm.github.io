Key ideas from the book that gave the world the Business Model Canvas: the nine blocks every business is made of, common patterns, and ways to design new models.

Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur wrote the book together with hundreds of practitioners from many countries. The main tool is the Business Model Canvas: one page that shows how a company creates, delivers and captures value.

## The nine blocks

- **Customer segments** — for whom we create value.
- **Value propositions** — which customer problems we solve and needs we meet.
- **Channels** — how we deliver value to the customer.
- **Customer relationships** — how we acquire and retain customers.
- **Revenue streams** — what customers pay for and how.
- **Key resources** — what the model needs to work.
- **Key activities** — what we must do well.
- **Key partnerships** — who helps us and what we rely on.
- **Cost structure** — what the model costs.

The right side of the canvas is about value and the customer; the left side is about efficiency.

## Common patterns

- **Unbundling** a business into customer relationships, product innovation and infrastructure.
- **The long tail** — selling many niche products in small volumes.
- **Multi-sided platforms** that bring several customer groups together.
- **Free as a business model** — freemium, advertising, bait and hook.
- **Open business models**, where a company collaborates with outside partners.

## How to design models

The authors suggest working with the canvas like designers: gathering customer insights, ideating, visual thinking, prototyping models, storytelling and playing out future scenarios.

## What a PM can take from it

Fill in the canvas before development: it quickly shows where the model doesn't add up — for example, the value is clear but there's no channel to the customer, or revenue doesn't cover key costs.

## Who should read it

PMs, founders and anyone who defends a new product to the business. The book is almost entirely visual and works well as a reference to come back to.
