In April 2022 MTS ran an independent assessment of its product managers; the testing was done by Assessty. At the time I was developing MTS ID — the single sign-on for MTS products.

<figure><img src="../assets/posts/assessty-mts.jpg" width="1200" height="750" alt="Fragment of the Assessty report: inclination — product creation, orientation — part of a product, grade Middle."><figcaption>First page of the Assessty report</figcaption></figure>

## Outcome

- **Grade: Middle.**
- **Inclination: "Product creation".** I'm most drawn to launching fundamentally new solutions under high risk and uncertainty rather than maintaining what already works. That matches what I did before and after: four products from scratch.
- **Orientation: "Part of a product".** Logical for MTS ID: it is functionality embedded into large MTS products.

## Strengths — 8–10 out of 10

10 out of 10: **value management**, **business model**, **risk management**, **sales funnel**.

8–9: key metrics, task grooming, monetisation models, qualitative research, hypothesis framing, UX testing, retention, content marketing, development methodology, Lean, timelines and planning, hiring, UI.

In "Vision and strategy" and "Backlog and development management" my score was above both the company and market average; in "Communication and sales" — above the company average.

## Personal qualities

In simulated situations, leadership potential, flexibility, sense-making and critical thinking showed most strongly.

## What I did with it

The report listed growth areas: product roadmap, quantitative research, A/B tests, unit economics, stakeholder management. I worked on them over the following years: the Asia Online relaunch was built on hypotheses and metrics and delivered +103% revenue, and at Gazprombank I defended the product concept and budget before stakeholders myself.
