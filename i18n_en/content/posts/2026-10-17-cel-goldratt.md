Key ideas from a business novel about a plant manager who has three months to save his factory — and about the theory of constraints, which has since been applied far beyond factories.

Eliyahu Goldratt was a physicist and the author of the theory of constraints. The Goal is written as a novel: plant manager Alex Rogo talks to his former teacher Jonah, who asks questions instead of giving answers.

## What a company's goal is

The goal is to make money now and in the future. Everything else — machine efficiency, staff utilisation, report quality — is only a means. Goldratt proposes three measures:

- **Throughput** — the rate at which the system generates money through sales;
- **Inventory** — the money invested in things the system intends to sell;
- **Operating expense** — the money spent turning inventory into throughput.

## The bottleneck decides everything

A system's output equals the output of its slowest link. An hour lost at the bottleneck is an hour lost for the whole system. An hour saved at a non-bottleneck is a mirage.

The scout hike scene explains this better than any diagram: the troop moves at the speed of the slowest boy, and the gaps grow because of statistical fluctuations and dependent events.

## Five focusing steps

1. Identify the system's constraint.
2. Exploit it to the maximum.
3. Subordinate everything else to it.
4. Elevate the constraint.
5. Go back to step one when the constraint moves — and don't let inertia become the new constraint.

## What a PM can take from it

Look for the bottleneck in the delivery process: reviews, testing, approvals, one overloaded role. Speeding up everything else is useless while the bottleneck stays. The same logic applies to a funnel: improve the step where you lose the most users.

## Who should read it

Anyone who manages processes and teams. The novel form makes it an easy read, and its ideas are worth discussing with your engineering team.
