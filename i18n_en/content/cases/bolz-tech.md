## Problem

- **Users:** in 60% of cases the sharing service installed on your phone isn't nearby — and using another means a new app, sign-up and card.
- **Sharing services:** negative unit economics, small marketing budgets, organic-only growth.
- **Partner apps:** looking for ways to raise MAU, frequency and loyalty, and to monetise users.

## Solution

bolz embeds into other apps via WebView: users rent any sharing service without installing a new app or signing up; one card works for all. Up to 30% of users with 24/7 sharing access come back to the host app 2–5 extra times a month. Business model: from 10% of each rental.

## What I did as CPO

- Ran a survey of 200 users in Russia and Moscow.
- Defined the product strategy and the B2B2C model.
- Designed the user journey and architecture: bolz web app inside partner apps, single backend, API connections to sharing services, business dashboard.
- Modelled unit economics for "first 100 paying users" and product-market fit (CAC 500–1,000 ₽, D30 retention 3–7%, LTV 3–7k ₽).
- Owned the roadmap: from power banks to bikes, scooters, cars, coworking and EV charging.

## What came next

We saw users increasingly arrive via AI assistants rather than apps — which led to [bolz.dev](bolz-dev.html), an MCP connector for real-world actions by AI agents.

## What I learned

- Embedding into partners' apps lets you grow without a marketing budget, but the cycle with each partner is long: half a year passed from partnership approval to production.
- It's better to start with one type of sharing (power banks) than with all of them at once.
- We noticed users increasingly come through AI assistants — and bolz.dev grew out of that.
