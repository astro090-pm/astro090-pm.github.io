## The idea: "help right into your hands"

A person in need leaves a request and gets help for free: cleaning and housework, small repairs, a nanny or tutor, a carer, professional repair work. The platform connects three sides:

- **recipient** — the person who needs help;
- **helper** — a volunteer or specialist who does the job;
- **donor** — someone who pays for the job when the helper isn't a volunteer.

## How it works

1. The recipient verifies their status via Gosuslugi (government services portal) and posts a task.
2. Helpers are notified and respond; the recipient picks one.
3. Donors are notified; one confirms funding.
4. The helper does the job and reports to the donor.
5. The donor accepts the work and confirms payment.

Dobster is non-profit: no commission, the donor's money goes straight to the task. Potential audience — 69 million people officially in socially vulnerable groups in Russia.

## Partnership programme

To attract donors and helpers we created a programme for companies — "talk about good, give good, do good": tell clients and staff about Dobster, reward the most active helpers and donors, fund tasks or volunteer. Partners get a partner block on the platform and a joint PR campaign.

## What I did

- Designed the partnership programme as an acquisition channel.

## What this case shows

A full launch in the social sector, where users are not the most digital audience and every transaction closes three sides at once.

## What I learned

- When three parties close a deal, each one's journey has to be designed separately. The hardest journey is the client's — an elderly person's.
- Attracting donors and helpers is a product in itself: that's how the partnership programme appeared.
